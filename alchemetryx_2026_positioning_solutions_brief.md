## 1. Why this document exists

The Fable draft was a strategy for a different company. It priced work at ten times what Alchemetryx sells, assumed delivery capacity that does not exist, and built its central argument on four regulations that were never enacted.

This brief keeps the roughly 20% that was sound, corrects the record, and rebuilds the offer on two things that are real: **dated government mandates** and **the gap between owning AI and having a system**.

Three decisions frame everything below.

| Decision | Position |
|---|---|
| Regulation's role | Outcome-based messaging. The mandates supply the deadline, not a new service line. Commercials unchanged. |
| Solution architecture | Two levels: AI Adoption and AI Usage. Sector-agnostic architecture, sector-anchored proof. |
| India | Own vertical, own trigger. Not a mirror of the UK. |

The correction log in Section 2 struck out invented adoption figures. Here is what the UK government itself published instead, in the SME Digital Adoption Taskforce's final report.

| Figure | Source detail |
|---|---|
| Over 5.5 million UK SMEs, 99.8% of the UK's business landscape | Taskforce final report |
| A 1% productivity uplift across SMEs could add £94 billion annually to UK GDP | Taskforce final report, five-year modelling |
| Firm-level productivity gains of 7 to 18% per technology adopted | Taskforce final report |
| Innovative SMEs recorded 14.8% revenue growth | Taskforce final report |

**Why this matters more than the number the draft invented.** The 43% figure is the honest size of Level 1. Close to half the UK SME population has explicitly decided against AI, and that share is shrinking by five points a year. That is not a market Alchemetryx has to persuade from zero. It is a market moving on its own, and the Adoption tier exists to catch it as it moves, not to convert it from a standing start.

The £94 billion GDP figure is a government number, not a vendor claim, and it is safe to use in outreach and on the site because it traces to a named report rather than an invented index. It answers "why now" at the policy level, sitting above the mandate-by-mandate urgency in Section 3.

## 5. The two-level solution architecture

Every prospect sits in one of two states. This is the routing logic behind the entire site and the first qualification question on every call.

```
                        Does the business already
                        run AI or automation?
                                 │
              ┌──────────────────┴──────────────────┐
              │ NO                                  │ YES
              ▼                                     ▼
     ┌─────────────────┐                  ┌─────────────────┐
     │   LEVEL 1       │                  │   LEVEL 2       │
     │   ADOPTION      │                  │   USAGE         │
     │                 │                  │                 │
     │ No system yet.  │                  │ Tools bought.   │
     │ Work runs on    │                  │ Nothing         │
     │ people and      │                  │ measurably      │
     │ spreadsheets.   │                  │ changed.        │
     └────────┬────────┘                  └────────┬────────┘
              │                                    │
              └───────────────┬────────────────────┘
                              ▼
                    Same three products.
                    Diagnostic → Build → Retainer
```

### Level 1: Adoption

**Who.** Owner-led UK firms, 25 to 150 staff, running on people, email and spreadsheets. They may have a Microsoft licence nobody uses. They have not bought AI or automation as a deliberate decision.

**What they believe.** "AI is for bigger companies." "We are not technical enough." "Our data is a mess."

**What they are actually afraid of.** Being the last one to move, and not knowing what good looks like.

**What Alchemetryx sells them.** One process that stops breaking, and the AI that makes it run. The Diagnostic finds the process, the Build makes it run, the Retainer keeps it running. AI is the engine here, not the headline. Lead with the process because that is the pain they feel every day, then show them the AI doing the part they currently do by hand. The order is deliberate. It is not a demotion.

**Message.** *Start with one process. AI is what makes it run without you.*

**Why this level exists at all.** Firms with no prior AI spend need a route in on their own terms. Excluding them at the entry gate hands them to a competitor who will take the meeting. This is that route.

### Level 2: Usage

**Who.** Firms with Copilot, ChatGPT licences, a Zapier account, maybe an automation project that a consultant delivered and left. Spend has happened. Return has not been measured.

**What they believe.** "We already have AI, the IT team handles it." Often said by someone who is unaware there is a problem to fix and unaware a real solution exists, not someone deflecting.

**What is actually true.** They have tools. They do not have a system. Nobody has ever measured whether any of it changed a number.

**What Alchemetryx sells them.** The audit that nobody else offers. Competitors sell readiness assessments, which ask whether you are ready to start. Alchemetryx asks whether what you already bought actually worked. That question has no established competitor and it is the sharper of the two levels commercially, because the budget line already exists.

**Message.** *You bought the AI. Did anything change?*

**Note on the objection you already hit.** A UK contact said: "we are using Co-Pilot as an AI tool which is maintained by IT team." That is not a rejection. That is a Level 2 prospect self-identifying. The answer is: "Good. What did it change? Show me the before and after number." Most cannot answer. That silence is the sale.

### How the levels map to what you sell

Prices are not published on the site, deliberately, unlike competitors who publish rate cards.

| Product | Level 1 framing | Level 2 framing | Price |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Diagnostic** | Find the one process costing you most | Audit what your AI spend actually returned | £3,500 |
| **The Build** | Make that process run without you | Rebuild the tool into a working system | £8,000 to £15,000 |
| **The Retainer** | Keep it running and add the next one | Keep measuring, keep tuning | £2,000 to £3,500 per month |

India: Diagnostic at ₹2,50,000 full or ₹1,25,000 single-function. Two fixed prices so negotiation moves scope, not price.

**Operating Intelligence Framework** stays as method IP for proposals only. It does not appear publicly.

## 6. Outcome-based messaging

Agreed position: the messaging leads with the client's end state. The commercials stay fixed. No outcome-based fees, no measured-result billing. That comes after twenty delivered engagements and a defensible benchmark, not at one case study.

### What replaces "Intelligence Operating System"

The draft's headline, "The Intelligence Operating System for SMEs That Refuse to Stay Small", is more abstract than the language that already failed in conversation. It is out.

**Recommended primary headline:**

> **One process. Running without you. In 90 days.**

It states an outcome, it is time-bound, it needs no explanation, and it works for both levels.

**Alternates to test:**

- *You bought the AI. We build the system that makes it pay.* Sharper for Level 2, excludes Level 1.
- *Your tools are not a system.* Names the Level 2 gap without naming AI. Weaker as a headline than the primary because it tells the reader what they lack rather than what they get.

**Sub-head:** We find the process costing you most, rebuild it so it runs on its own, and stay to prove it worked.

**Single CTA:** Book a 30-minute call. UK enquiries route to Nimish, India to Ashok, shared calendar. No second CTA competing with it.

### Language rules

Banned on the site and in outreach, because they caused the failure already recorded:

- "Operations" as a standalone noun. Say the actual process: rota, invoicing, onboarding, quoting, claims.
- "Intelligence", "operating system", "transformation", "unlock", "empower", "seamless".
- Any adjective doing work a number should do.

Required: name the process, name the time it takes now, name what it costs. Owner-led buyers think in hours and money, not capabilities.

## 7. Proof architecture

This is the binding constraint. Not positioning, not the site. Proof.

**What exists today.** Care Rota, cleared for publication. That is one. The Diversity Festival build (Martine Eni, SOW-DF-2026-001) has a case study document in brand format. The physio engagement is unpublished. Fitosys is a fourth source, and a different kind: it is Alchemetryx's own India-market product, not a client engagement, so it proves the method was used on Alchemetryx's own risk before being sold to anyone else. That is a legitimate answer to "have you done this yourselves", which a buyer will ask. It should be labelled clearly as an own-build case, not dressed up as a client story.

**Day-one requirement.** Care Rota published in full before the site goes live. A site with a strong argument and no proof is what you have now, and it has produced zero replies.

**Target for the next 90 days.** Four published proofs, not fifty. Care Rota, Diversity Festival, Fitosys, and one new client engagement. Fitosys needs no client sign-off, so it is the fastest one to get live and should not wait behind the others.

**Format per case study.** One page. The situation, the number before, what was built, the number after, and the client's own words. No video requirement, no infographic. Written proof that a stranger can read in two minutes.

**Trust items that are real and should be shown:** UK company registration number 17199377. Its absence from the live site reads as a red flag to a UK buyer. Add it to the footer.

**Certifications:** Cyber Essentials is worth pursuing because UK buyers ask for it and it is achievable at your size. ISO 27001 and SOC 2 as listed in the draft are not realistic and should not be promised.

## 8. Site architecture, day one

Seven industry pages, three blog posts a week and two webinars a month is a six-person marketing team. That is out. This is what actually gets built.

```
Home
├── The two questions (Level 1 / Level 2 router)
├── What we do
│   ├── The Diagnostic
│   ├── The Build
│   └── The Retainer
├── Proof (3 case studies)
├── About (Ashok, Nimish, Pravin, company number, addresses)
├── Book a call (Fillout)
└── India (own page, e-invoicing trigger, own proof)
```

**Home page sequence:**

1. Headline and single CTA
2. The two questions. "Have you bought AI yet?" routes to Level 1 or Level 2 content in place. This is the router and it does the work that seven industry pages were supposed to do.
3. What actually happens. Diagnostic, Build, Retainer in plain language with timeframes, no prices.
4. Proof. Three case studies, real numbers.
5. Who we are. Faces, company number, both addresses. UK buyers check this.
6. The deadline block. Whichever mandate is closest, stated plainly with the date. Updated quarterly.
7. CTA repeated.

**Stack.** Next.js on Hostinger from Git, Fillout for booking. No personalisation engine, no 6sense, no Demandbase, no Segment, no Mutiny. An enterprise martech stack is the wrong spend for a business with no traffic yet. Google Analytics 4 and Search Console are sufficient until there is something to measure.
